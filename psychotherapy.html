What does Psychotherapy do?

Psychotherapy, also known as talk therapy, is a collaborative process where we explore your thoughts, feelings, and behaviors. The work can help you deepen insight, develop healthier coping strategies, and make meaningful changes guided by your goals, values, and circumstances.

What to Expect

In our sessions, we work together to identify your goals and create a personalized plan. Through open dialogue, practical strategies, and self-reflection, we address challenges while building a clear path forward.

Session 1: The Intake

We get to know each other and explore what has brought you to therapy. A psychosocial assessment helps establish the foundation for our work.

Sessions 2–4: Building Trust

The early sessions emphasize safety, trust, and understanding your perspective while beginning to introduce useful coping skills.

Sessions 5 and Beyond: The Work May Begin

As the therapeutic foundation becomes stronger, the work may move deeper into reflection, processing past hurts, addressing fears, and setting future goals.

FAQs in Psychotherapy

Is everything I say in therapy confidential?

Confidentiality is a cornerstone of therapy, with limited legal exceptions such as certain safety-related situations. These exceptions are reviewed as part of informed consent.

What if I don’t feel a connection with you?

Therapeutic fit matters. It is appropriate to discuss concerns about connection or fit, and referrals can be considered when another therapist may be a better match.

Can I do therapy if I’m an introvert or not good at expressing myself?

Yes. Therapy can adapt to different communication styles and proceed at a pace that feels workable for you.

I’m looking for support for my loved one. What should I do?

You can encourage them to contact a mental health professional and offer practical support in exploring options. You may also seek your own consultation about how to support them effectively.
